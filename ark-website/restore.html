The desert is not broken. It is a living system running on a different clock, and it answers when you learn its hours. This planner holds what Dawn has measured with her own hands at Ark Unit 1, kept strictly apart from what is still being tested. Read both. Trust the difference.

Start With Water

Every restoration here begins the same way, because the desert punishes anyone who starts with plants. First, learn how water moves across your ground. Cool the refuge, not the whole sky. Shade before machines. Let the shape of each watering point follow its relationship to the life drinking from it. A chicken, a horse, and a human do not drink the same way, and the system should not pretend they do.

Measured

The middle tree

Three trees, same water, one morning. The two she gave attention and energy to thrived. The one she neglected wilted, leaves drooping. When she put intention into the water hitting it, the leaves came back up. One morning, three trees, no laboratory, no control group. She wrote down exactly what she saw.

Measured

The poultry water node

Built and running: a raised circular drinking ring for chickens, with a protected center, gentle circulation, an ice well for extreme heat, automatic refill, filtration, and a low-point purge that sends old water to plants instead of the dirt. It started with three chickens and taught her how to build an artery.

Now Testing

The honest line: everything below is under trial at Ark Unit 1, or planned for trial. None of it is a result yet. Hypotheses live here until the dirt confirms or corrects them, and if something does not work, that gets written down too.
Testing

Hugelkultur beds

Under construction since October 2026: cardboard, then mulch, then dried firewood, then dirt on top, then planting. Buried wood as a slow sponge, holding water and feeding fungi for years. No results yet. The beds are young.

Testing

Magnetics on irrigation water

Planned with controls and measured growth: magnets on the water path, against untreated water, same source, same plants. Peer-reviewed studies suggest it helps germination and yield. Unit 1 will find out for itself.

Pallet fencing from the hardware store

Planned: the local hardware store sets aside free and discarded pallets, cut in half, built into fencing around the gardens. Wind filter and shade edge from what was headed for the dumpster.

Radiative-cooling fabrics

On watch, not yet ordered: new fabrics that reflect sunlight and shed heat to the sky with no power, tested on a desert greenhouse in Kashgar. Heat is the binding constraint here. Worth pricing the moment it can be bought.
