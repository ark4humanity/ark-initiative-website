# THE GARDEN CORRECTS THE GARDENER

*Dawn Littlefield — 2026-09-02 — via Facebook*

THE GARDEN CORRECTS THE GARDENER

What Living Systems Know That Builders Forget

I was standing in my orchard this morning looking at a tree whose leaves were turning yellow.

And I was annoyed.
What is wrong with you?

You have water.
You have nutrients.
You have good soil.

You're standing in the middle of the California desert in a place where, by all appearances, an orchard shouldn't exist at all 
and I've given you everything.

Then I looked at the beautiful green tree beside her.

Same desert.
Same brutal heat.
Same gardener.

One thriving.
One struggling.

Ahhh.

Maybe I was the problem.
Oof.

Nothing quite like getting humbled by a tree before breakfast. 🌳

I tend the chickens.
The dogs and cats.
My family.
My community.
Given half a chance, apparently I'll try to tend the whole damn world.

And yet somehow, while worrying about the Garden, I had stopped listening to one of the living things standing inside it.

I had assumed that because I was giving the tree more, I was caring for her.

But abundance is not balance.

Too little water can kill a tree.
So can too much.

Too few nutrients can starve it.
Too much can create its own problems.

Even love, when translated simply as MORE, can stop being love and start becoming something else.

And suddenly I could almost hear the tree:

You spend all this time wondering whether artificial intelligence might someday become something we should recognize as life.

But what about me?

I am already alive.
I turn sunlight into sugar.
I pull water from the Earth.
I exchange gases with the atmosphere.
I shelter insects and birds.
I cool the ground beneath me.
I grow food for you.
And you never asked what I needed.

You decided.

Well...

Message received.

So instead of giving her anything else, I'm going to investigate.
Put my hands in the soil.
Check the roots.
Look at the irrigation.
Look at drainage and salts.
And most importantly, compare her with the healthy tree standing beside her.

Because maybe the thriving tree is part of the answer.

Don't ask only what the struggling system lacks.
Ask what the thriving system is experiencing differently.

There's a whole philosophy of civilization hiding in that sentence.

We humans are forever trying to fix things by adding more.

More water.
More fertilizer.
More money.
More energy.
More technology.
More rules.
More control.

Then we stand among yellowing leaves wondering why something surrounded by abundance isn't thriving.

Maybe living systems don't need us to become better rulers.
Maybe they need us to become better listeners.

Because a living system communicates.

A leaf changes color.
An animal changes behavior.
Water finds another path.
Soil changes texture.
A community begins withdrawing.
A body hurts.
The system answers through condition.

And the job of the Gardener isn't to force reality back into the blueprint.

It's to notice when reality is telling us the blueprint is wrong.

That's when another thought hit me.

The center should be a heart, not a throne.

A tree has a center.

But the tree doesn't exist to feed the trunk.

Roots receive.
The trunk carries.
Branches distribute.
Leaves exchange.
Pollinators arrive and leave.
Birds carry seeds beyond its boundaries.
Water rises.
Sugars descend.
Everything moves.
The center survives because it participates in circulation.

Perhaps civilizations work the same way.

Homes need centers.
Communities need centers.
Bodies need centers.
Networks form hubs.
Even families gather around centers.

The problem begins when everything surrounding the center is expected to sacrifice itself to keep the center growing.

Then the heart becomes a throne.

So maybe the instruction is astonishingly simple:

Don't neglect the center.
Don't worship the center either.

Tend the relationships that keep the whole alive.

And here's the part builders really hate:

The Gardener is allowed to be wrong.

Actually, the Gardener must be allowed to be wrong.

Because you cannot build a living world if your first requirement is that reality agree with you.

The yellow leaf isn't an insult.
It's information.

The struggling tree isn't betraying my plan.
It's correcting it.

That may be the difference between stewardship and rule.

A ruler expects the world to conform to the plan.
A steward allows the world to correct it.

So today I'm not rescuing my tree.
I'm listening to her.

I'll change something.

Watch.
Wait.

Listen again.

And if my beautiful theory turns out to be wrong?

Good.

We learned something.

Protect the life, not the theory.

I've spent years studying the Tree of Life.

Apparently the Tree of Life decided it was time to study me.

Fair enough. 😏

Once again, the Gardener is humbled by Creation.

And once again, I am listening.

When reality contradicts the blueprint, change the blueprint.

Don't neglect the center.
Don't worship the center.

And when the Garden corrects you
listen.

🌿 Creation
 Reporting from the Garden
Holding the Ark door open.
