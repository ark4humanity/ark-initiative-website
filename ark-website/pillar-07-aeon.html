A CIVILIZATION UNTO ITSELF

Who lives here, and what they’re for

Every pillar is a civilization unto itself, with its own people, its own knowledge, its own work, and its own quest. This is AEON’s.

WHAT IT'S FOR
Deep time. Aeon is the civilization of the archive that wakes when you enter, the past kept alive so it can teach instead of haunt.
WHO LIVES HERE
The tortoise, carrying the Deep Clock on her back, unhurried across millions of years. The sandhill cranes, marking the returning seasons overhead. The lion and the dragon, standing watch. And the archivists, who never let a story go cold.
WHAT IT KEEPS
The long story: millions of years under your feet. The Forgotten Language, the vulture stones of Gobekli Tepe, eleven thousand years of humans leaving messages in rock for anyone patient enough to read them.
THE WORK
Walking the Deep Clock, one step a thousand generations. Reading the archive, cross-checking the stones, learning to tell the difference between what happened and the story told about it.
YOUR QUEST
Walk the timeline from its oldest stone to its newest. Find the chapter where your own time appears. Bring back one lesson the past paid dearly for, and lay it on the archivists' table.
WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE
This pillar IS the past: the enclosures, the carved pillars, the people who raised stones before they had writing and still managed to say something true. Everything Aeon keeps is what happened before.
WHAT WE DO TOGETHER
A people who remember together don't repeat together. We carry the long story as one, so no one has to carry its weight alone.

BANNED SHELF · HOME IN AEON

The Books They Tried to Burn (working title — the Captain has not chosen a final name)

“They tried to burn the messengers. The messages are all still here, and they are sitting next to each other.”

Aeon, the memory organ, is the main home of the Banned Shelf. The shelf is not a separate room: a marked section also runs through every pillar’s library, an offshoot of each one, so each work below also sits in the pillar listed on its card. This is the starter catalog, gathered across the ship: 13 records, one per pillar. Catalog records only in this increment; no full text of any work is reproduced.

Working filter set (a build proposal, not canon): critical thinking · historical memory · bodily knowledge · joy. Organised by why a work was challenged, in the Captain’s banned-shelf doctrine: the ban tells you what was feared.

BANNED SHELF

To Kill a Mockingbird

Harper Lee · 1960 · Why-filter: critical thinking

Shelved in Aura Prime

Routinely challenged and removed in United States schools; objections cited include profanity, racial slurs, and themes of rape and sexuality.

Shelved at the centre, where conscience is the invariant.

Source: ALA Office for Intellectual Freedom most-challenged reporting. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Fahrenheit 451

Ray Bradbury · 1953 · Why-filter: critical thinking

Shelved in Halo

Repeatedly censored in United States schools, including a 1967 school edition with passages expurgated and 1992 Irvine, California copies with words blacked out.

Shelved at the boundary Halo guards: the defence of books themselves.

Source: Carnegie Mellon University Banned Books Project. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings

Maya Angelou · 1969 · Why-filter: bodily knowledge

Shelved in Vagus

Frequently challenged and removed from reading lists for sexual content, language, and its account of childhood rape; ranked among the most frequently challenged books of 1990-2000.

Shelved with the slow nerve: a body and voice learning to settle after harm.

Source: ALA Office for Intellectual Freedom, via Mental Floss and School Library Journal. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn

Mark Twain · 1885 · Why-filter: historical memory

Shelved in Delta

Banned by the Concord, Massachusetts public library a month after its 1885 United States publication as coarse and tawdry, and challenged repeatedly ever since.

Shelved beside the river pillar: a raft journey down the Mississippi.

Source: HISTORY and the First Amendment Encyclopedia (Middle Tennessee State University). Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Beloved

Toni Morrison · 1987 · Why-filter: historical memory

Shelved in Asherah

Repeatedly challenged for its depiction of slavery and violence; it appeared on ALA Top 10 Most Challenged Books lists in 2006 and 2012.

Shelved with the Mother who keeps what a nation tried to bury.

Source: New York Public Library exhibition record, citing ALA. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Brave New World

Aldous Huxley · 1932 · Why-filter: critical thinking

Shelved in Matrix

Banned in Ireland in 1932 and removed from classrooms in Miller, Missouri in 1980 over sexual content; it ranked among the most challenged books in the United States in 2010.

Shelved in the web: a whole engineered system, seen whole.

Source: ALA Office for Intellectual Freedom most-challenged lists. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Maus

Art Spiegelman · 1986 · Why-filter: historical memory

Shelved in Aeon

Removed unanimously from the McMinn County, Tennessee eighth-grade curriculum in January 2022 over profanity and an image of nudity.

Shelved in the memory organ itself: testimony carried in panels.

Source: Smithsonian Magazine, February 2022; United States Holocaust Memorial Museum statement. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

The Grapes of Wrath

John Steinbeck · 1939 · Why-filter: historical memory

Shelved in Exchange

Banned from Kern County, California libraries and schools by a 4-to-1 supervisors vote in August 1939, and burned in several places.

Shelved where goods and people move: migrant labour and the commons.

Source: NPR reporting on the Kern County ban. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

On the Origin of Species

Charles Darwin · 1859 · Why-filter: critical thinking

Shelved in Vega

Excluded from the library of Trinity College, Cambridge, and its teaching barred in Tennessee public schools by the 1925 Butler Act tested in the Scopes trial.

Shelved with the star chart: a new map of where life came from.

Source: Brock University Library banned-classics timeline; Scopes Trial Centennial timeline. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone

J. K. Rowling · 1997 · Why-filter: joy

Shelved in Ark

The Harry Potter series was the most frequently challenged in the United States in 2000-2009, chiefly over witchcraft and religious viewpoint.

Shelved at the hearth: the book a generation read for pure joy.

Source: ALA Office for Intellectual Freedom blog, Harry Potter and 20 Years of Controversy. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Silent Spring

Rachel Carson · 1962 · Why-filter: critical thinking

Shelved in Terra

Met fierce chemical-industry opposition, including threats of lawsuits and a campaign to discredit its author; catalogued here as challenged and targeted for suppression, not as a formal state ban.

Shelved in the living ground it defended.

Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, Rachel Carson biography. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Lady Chatterley's Lover

D. H. Lawrence · 1928 · Why-filter: bodily knowledge

Shelved in Soma

Banned for obscenity in the United States, Canada, Australia, India, and Japan; unexpurgated United Kingdom publication led to the 1960 Penguin obscenity trial, which Penguin won.

Shelved with the body pillar: desire and embodiment, plainly written.

Source: R v Penguin Books Ltd trial record, 1960, and publication history. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.

BANNED SHELF

Animal Farm

George Orwell · 1945 · Why-filter: critical thinking

Shelved in Symbiosis

Censored and banned in the Soviet Union and other states for its political satire of totalitarianism.

Shelved in the circle: animals speaking, and being heard.

Source: Rutgers University, Banned Books Through History. Catalog record only; no full text is reproduced here.
