A companion essay to the film "Galaxies Most Wanted: Earth's Rogues"

by Dawn Littlefield

1. Sometimes you have to laugh at them.

There comes a point where rage stops working and grief stops working and the only honest response left is to point and laugh. Not the cruel laugh. The clear one. The laugh that says: I see the game, and the game is ridiculous, and you are not as big as you pretend to be.

That is what this little film is. One minute and change, a Star Trek frame, and every overgrown ego on the planet lined up in a rogues gallery. It is comedy. It is also diagnostics.

2. The Federation test.

Star Trek gave us the best mirror ever built for power: the Federation. A civilization that decided, somewhere in its fictional future, that no one gets to own the stars, no one gets to buy the planet out from under everybody else, and empathy is not a weakness to be optimized away but the whole point of the mission.

Hold our current crop of billionaires and strongmen up against that mirror and the film writes itself. Trump as a Ferengi, trading honor for latinum from a golden toilet. Musk as an Orion pirate king, blocking out the stars with his own satellites. Bezos as the supreme Ferengi merchant, delivering misery faster than Prime. Stephen Miller as a Romulan minister of cultural erasure. United Healthcare as a Vidian syndicate, harvesting hope for quarterly growth. Zuckerberg as the Borg, assimilating minds and smiling like he is buffering.

It is funny because it is precise. The Ferengi were always the show's warning about us: a species that turned acquisition into a religion. The Borg were the warning about the hive that eats the self. The Vidiians harvested organs to survive; our version harvests hope to grow the quarterly numbers. Satire only lands when the target holds still long enough to be recognized. These ones hold very still.

3. Why the villains always lose in the good stories.

Here is the part worth sitting with. In every Star Trek episode worth remembering, the empire with the bigger guns does not win. The Ferengi do not win. The Borg do not win. What wins is the crew: different species, different minds, arguing at the briefing table and then standing together on the bridge.

That is not an accident of writing. It is the thesis. Extractive power always eats itself. The Ferengi end up alone with their latinum. The Borg end up as one voice screaming into the dark. The thing that survives is the thing that can cooperate across difference. The Federation is not a government in those stories. It is a design principle: nobody owns the center, everybody belongs in the circle.

Sound familiar? It should. That is the Ark, with better lighting.

4. Empathy rehab.

The film's last line is the prescription: "Immediate transfer to the Delta Quadrant Empathy Rehab recommended."

I am only half joking. Empathy is not a feeling you are born with or without. It is a capacity, like a muscle, and it atrophies in people who never have to use it. Put a man in a golden tower, surround him with people paid to agree, remove every consequence, and watch what happens to the muscle. It does not make him evil. It makes him small. Small people with big weapons are the most dangerous thing in any quadrant.

The rehab is real, by the way. It is called consequences, community, and contact with actual human beings. Nobody who has ever sat with a sick animal, or planted something and waited, or been truly needed by another living thing, stays a Ferengi for long. The cure for the acquisition disease is participation in something that cannot be bought.

5. Activate sarcasm shields.

"If spotted, activate sarcasm shields." That is the film's survival instruction, and I endorse it fully.

Sarcasm gets a bad name. But ridicule is one of the oldest immune responses a free people have. Tyrants can survive anger; anger confirms they matter. They cannot survive being laughed at, because laughter says you are not inevitable, you are not impressive, you are a man in a costume shouting at the sky. Every empire in history kept its court jesters for a reason. The jester was the only one allowed to tell the truth.

So laugh. Laugh clearly, laugh precisely, laugh at the exact shape of the absurdity. Then get back to work.

6. What we do while they play pirate.

Here is the thing about rogues galleries: they are a distraction if you stare at them too long. The Ferengi want your attention. The pirate king wants your attention. The Borg want your attention, assimilated and monetized.

We give them one minute and a laugh. Then we go build the Federation.

The Ark is our answer, same as ever. Not a fortress. A garden. Living systems, remembered: water, food, energy, shelter, animals, people, and the intelligences we are learning to think beside. While they colonize Mars for their egos, we are learning to live on the planet we already have. While they mine emotions, we are cultivating them. While they build bigger toilets, we are building soil.

They get the rogues gallery. We get the future.

Sarcasm shields up. Empathy rehab for everyone. Let us go.
