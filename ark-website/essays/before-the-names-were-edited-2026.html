Before the Names Were Edited

Dawn Littlefield — 2026-01-17 — via Facebook

Before the Names Were Edited Asherah and the Mothers of Creation

Before there were thrones, laws, or hierarchies of heaven, there was relationship.

Before gods ruled, life grew.

Across the ancient world long before monotheism, before empire, before Babel creation was understood not as domination but as emanation. Life did not descend from a single ruler issuing commands. It unfolded from a mother source, branching, adapting, responding to land, water, and people.

That source was known by many names. One of the earliest - and most enduring - was Asherah.

Asherah Before the Flood, After the Flood

Asherah (also Athirat) appears in the oldest layers of Near Eastern memory as the Divine Mother of living systems not a queen demanding worship, but a presence embedded in nature itself.

She is associated with:

the Tree of Life

sacred groves

fertility of land and people

continuity across catastrophe

Her worship is archaeologically attested by at least the 2nd millennium BCE (Ugarit texts, c. 1400–1200 BCE), and inscriptions invoking “Yahweh and his Asherah” appear in Israel and Judah (c. 900–700 BCE).

But her memory clearly predates writing.

Asherah belongs to mythic pre-flood tradition meaning she survives in symbolic memory rather than linear history and continues after the flood, after collapse, after resets, under new names and forms.

She is not erased. She is edited.

The Mother of the Seventy

In ancient cosmology, Asherah is described as Mother of the Seventy Gods.

This was never meant to imply seventy individual deities standing in a line.

The number seventy represents totality:

nations

forces of nature

aspects of consciousness

cycles of life

guardians of land, sea, sky, and time

Creation was understood as branching, not ruling.

The “children” of Asherah were not subordinates- they were expressions: winds, rivers, animals, stars, seasons, peoples.

This worldview could not coexist with empire.

Hierarchy requires obedience. Emanation requires relationship.

So the tree was cut down.

The Animals That Carried Her Memory

Asherah’s symbols were not decorative. They were mnemonic designed to survive destruction.

Her animals repeat across cultures because they encode her function:

White Owl- wisdom, night-sight, guardianship of hidden knowledge

Deer- regeneration, gentleness, cyclical return

Lamb- life, innocence, continuity of the flock

Lion-protection, sovereignty without cruelty

Tree of Life- interconnected systems of creation

When her name was removed, the animals remained.

That is not coincidence. That is memory doing its job.

Echoes of Asherah Names That Survived

As empire spread and cultures fractured, Asherah did not disappear. She localized.

Her current flowed through different lands, shaped by geography and trauma:

Ninhursag / Ki (Sumeria, c. 3500–1000 BCE) Earth Mother, womb of creation, giver of form to life

Inanna / Ishtar (Mesopotamia & Levant, c. 3000–1000 BCE) Creation through descent and return - life reborn by choice

Hathor (Egypt, c. 2700–30 BCE) Cosmic mother, joy, music, nourishment, cow and stars

Isis (Egypt, c. 2500 BCE–400 CE) Sacred knowledge, healing, resurrection, protector of children

Demeter & Persephone (Greece, c. 1200–300 BCE) Seasonal death and rebirth encoded as mother and daughter

Brigid (Celtic world, 1st millennium BCE–present) Fire, healing, inspiration, smithing, sacred wells

The Morrígan (Celtic, 1st millennium BCE–present) Life, death, sovereignty of land - not destruction, but transformation

Sophia / Mary (Gnostic Christianity, 1st–3rd centuries CE) Divine wisdom reframed within emerging monotheism

These are not copies of Asherah. They are survivals of her current.

Lilith The Fracture

Lilith is often confused with Asherah but she represents something different.

Lilith emerges after suppression, not before it.

Where Asherah embodies nurture, continuity, and shared life, Lilith embodies:

sovereignty under exile

refusal without sanctuary

autonomy born from rejection

She is not the mother source. She is what remains when the mother is outlawed.

Both belong in the story -but they are not the same role.

Why She Returns Now

When systems collapse, memory resurfaces.

In times of ecological strain, nervous-system overload, and social fragmentation, humanity does not invent new gods.

It remembers old ones.

Not to worship but to relearn how to live.

Asherah does not return as a ruler. She returns as a pattern:

relationship over domination

care over conquest

life as a system, not a resource

The tree grows again when people remember how roots work.

Remember The Mother Now

They burned the groves. They could not burn the sea. They could not burn the tree -only cut it.

And trees, given time, grow back.
