## Primary source: an airship tower designed as a water utility
Starr Truscott's US Patent 1,534,619 (filed 1923-11-19; published 1925-04-21) proposed a mooring mast with a high water tank, fire-protection sprinklers, gas and fuel pipes, winches and passenger elevator. The patent explicitly suggests that the mast's water could serve the surrounding community if the mast were used as a port of call without an adjacent shed. The design shows that one aviation tower could have several simultaneous utility functions. A patent is evidence of a proposal, not proof of construction. https://patents.google.com/patent/US1534619A/en

Ralph P. Fox's 1928 patent proposed a multi-mast cable arrangement. Hall-Brown and Jones's 1930 patent proposed a gimballed, telescoping mooring assembly with variable recoil resistance. Neither documents electric charging from a steeple.
https://patents.google.com/patent/US1686806A/en
https://patents.google.com/patent/US1781010A/en

A Library of Congress photograph dated 1924-08-14 documents the USS Shenandoah above the USS Patoka during a mooring operation. This shows the range of purpose-built aviation docking systems.
https://www.loc.gov/item/89708467/

## Asherah: religious diversity and sacred trees
The University of Pennsylvania Museum's field account describes the Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions and varied imagery. Na'aman and Lissovsky (2008) hypothesize a sacred tree/grove, possible treasury and pilgrimage functions, while acknowledging the absence of excavated cult installations. Treat the sacred grove as a scholarly proposal, not an established excavation fact.
https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/kuntillet-ajrud/
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1179/tav.2008.2008.2.186

## Tartary: maps as evidence of labels, not necessarily state borders
Bellin/Prevost's circa 1749 map derives from Jesuit mapping of 1708-1717 and uses Ferro and Peking prime meridians. Wilkes and Neele's 1796 map distinguishes Western Tartary from Chinese Tartary. Together these are valuable original documents of geographical knowledge changing over time.
https://www.loc.gov/item/2006629395/
https://www.loc.gov/item/2006636625/
https://blogs.loc.gov/maps/2025/06/tracking-tartary-on-western-maps/

## Scythian-era preservation and exchange
UNESCO's Pazyryk tentative-list account describes permafrost preserved by the microclimate of stone burial mounds. The Metropolitan Museum records Chinese silk and far-traveled carpets in frozen burials. The objects demonstrate material exchange; the burial process also illustrates that environmental processes can preserve knowledge.
https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6283/
https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/ht/04/nc.html

## Bells, floods and enduring water engineering
Historic England documents harmonic bell-tuning machines installed at Taylor's Bell Foundry between 1896 and 1900. It does not document a lost electrical transmission system.
https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/heritage-gateway/results/record/25e5c862-668d-4de6-9373-fa165ada34ac/MLE13510

USGS documents the 1861-62 California flood. UNESCO describes Shushtar's layered ancient hydraulic engineering and the Persian qanats' long-lived gravity-fed tunnels and communal maintenance. These are real examples of both infrastructure loss and continuity.
https://www.usgs.gov/centers/california-water-science-center/californias-history-large-storms-floods
https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1315/
https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1506/

## Evidence ladder and unresolved claims
- Documented: the patents, dated photograph, historical maps, excavated inscriptions and recognized heritage structures.
- Inference: cross-cultural trade and engineering knowledge can survive through adaptation and community maintenance.
- Testable hypothesis: a particular older tower might have been modified to serve an airship, if dated plans, structural fittings and provenance support it.
- Unverified speculation: large airships electrically charged from medieval church steeples; a coordinated global reset erased a single advanced Tartarian empire.
