PART I
The Ones Who Tried to Set Us Free

Nikola Tesla
The Visionary They Bankrupted

Tesla’s Wardenclyffe Tower was designed to transmit free, wireless electricity to the entire world.
In his own words, he intended to create a “World Wireless System,” powered by the Earth and sky.

But financier J.P. Morgan pulled funding when he asked

> “If anyone can draw power freely, where do we put the meter?”

No meter = no money.
The tower was dismantled. Tesla died alone, in debt, his papers seized by the U.S. government.

That was no accident.
 That was a hit job on the future.

Joseph Newman
The Motor That Shouldn’t Exist

Newman invented an electromagnetic motor that allegedly produced over-unity energy-more out than in.
His patent was denied.
He was dragged through court for decades.
Scientists were split-some calling it fraud, others demanding real investigation.

What mattered wasn’t the result.

What mattered was this They made an example out of him.

Stanley Meyer
The Water Fuel Car

Meyer created a “water fuel cell” that split water into hydrogen and oxygen efficiently enough to run a car on water.
He was offered billions to sell out and shut up.
He refused.

In 1998, after a meeting with two investors, he sprinted from a restaurant shouting,

> “They poisoned me!”

He died on the spot. The coroner ruled it a brain aneurysm.
His technology vanished. His prototype cars were disassembled.

Another voice silenced. Another revolution aborted.

The Legal Tools of Suppression

The Invention Secrecy Act of 1951

Still in effect.
Still active.

It allows the U.S. government to place “secrecy orders” on any patent application deemed a threat to “national security”-including inventions that could destabilize economic systems.

According to the Federation of American Scientists, 5,784 patents were under secrecy orders as of 2017.
Many were in the categories of energy, propulsion, and advanced electronics.

One inventor (whose name is redacted) filed a patent for a high-efficiency power device in 2008.
It was sealed.
Never heard from again.

The “Economic Stability” Clause

Most assume secrecy orders apply only to weapons.
But the clause also applies to inventions that may threaten dominant industries-especially the energy sector.

Translation?
If your device could kill the oil industry, decentralize energy, or make utilities obsolete… it disappears.
