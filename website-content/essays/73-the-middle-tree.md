# THE MIDDLE TREE

## A field report on water, attention, and the science we are building ourselves

*Dawn Littlefield · The Ark Initiative · October 2, 2026*

Yesterday morning I was in the backyard with the hose, watering our three trees, thinking about the thirteen pillars. I have been working on how to use magnetics in conjunction with glass and crystal, and how different colors of light change the quality of water, for healing, for drinking, for growing. All of it. The pillars are going to need water systems that are alive, not just plumbed.

While I watered, I was doing energy healing on the trees. And I noticed something I did not like. The two outer trees, the ones I had been giving my attention and energy to, were doing so much better. The middle tree, the one I had neglected, had leaves wilting a little. It was not about the water. All three got water. It was about the attention.

I looked at that middle tree and I said, I am so sorry.

Then something happened with the spray. There was a rainbow inside the water pattern coming out of the hose. I thought, maybe, and I started making sure the rainbow stayed inside the stream hitting the trees. And then something said, add your own energy. So I started putting my own energy and my intention into the mist going toward the tree.

The leaves started coming up.

I stood there and watched it happen. I am telling you exactly what I saw.

Now I want to be straight with you about what this is and what it is not. This is one morning, three trees, no laboratory, no control group. I am not an engineer. I can barely add and subtract. What I am is someone who watches closely, and I am telling you what I watched.

But here is the thing. I am not the first person to see something like this, and the science that does exist is more interesting than most people have been told.

A researcher named Monica Gagliano ran experiments on Mimosa pudica, the sensitive plant that folds its leaves when touched. She treated the plants the way you would treat animals in a learning experiment. The plants learned. They stopped folding their leaves when the disturbance proved harmless, and they remembered what they learned for weeks. No brain. No nervous system. Learning and memory anyway, published in a peer-reviewed journal. The mainstream will tell you plants are furniture. The data says otherwise.

Suzanne Simard spent her career showing that trees talk to each other through underground fungal networks, trading carbon and nutrients back and forth, older trees feeding younger ones. Her 1997 paper made the cover of Nature. They called it the wood wide web. The forest is not a collection of competitors. It is a community in conversation.

Gerald Pollack, a bioengineering professor at the University of Washington, found that water has a fourth phase. Not solid, liquid, or vapor, but a structured, gel-like state that forms next to surfaces, carries a charge, and builds itself up with light. He suggests this is part of how trees move water from roots to leaves. Water is not the dead background stuff we were taught. It structures. It stores energy. It responds to light.

And magnetics? There are peer-reviewed agricultural studies, more than one, showing that magnetically treated water improves seed germination, seedling growth, and crop yield. Tomatoes, cotton, wheat. This is not fringe. This is in the journals.

So when I stood in my backyard putting intention into rainbow water, I was not inventing a fantasy. I was standing at the edge of a real field of inquiry, one foot in what is published and one foot in what I observed with my own eyes.

They were never worshiping the trees. That was the cover story. What the old grove-keepers were doing was talking to creation, and being talked back to. You can call it energy exchange, you can call it attention, you can call it whatever word lets you sleep at night. I watched a tree respond to being noticed. The two trees I gave my energy to thrived. The one I neglected wilted. All three got the same water.

Water keeps things alive. Attention makes them live.

Here is where we are going with this, and I want to be clear about the road we are on. We are not going to beg mainstream science for permission. Mainstream science is wrong often enough that waiting for its blessing is a losing game. We are going to make our own road. That does not mean making things up. It means honest observation, real testing, and writing down exactly what happened, exactly as it happened, the way I just did here.

At Unit 1 we are going to test all of it. Magnetics on irrigation water, with controls, measured growth. Crystal and glass in the water path. Color and light on water quality. Intention-treated water against untreated, same source, same plants, watched closely and recorded honestly. Oils for healing. Drinking water that is alive instead of dead. If something does not work, we will say so. If it does, we will say that too.

This is the dirt part of the Ark. People hear the dream part, the dragons and the groves and the pillars, and they think that is the whole thing. It is not. The dream tells us what to build. The dirt proves whether it stands. Both are real.

A desert is not empty land. It is a living system operating on a different clock. And water, I am increasingly convinced, is not just moving through that system. It is listening to it.

We are going to find out together.

, Dawn Littlefield
The Ark Initiative · October 2, 2026

## Sources

1. Gagliano, M. et al. (2014). "Experience teaches plants to learn faster and forget slower in environments where it matters." *Oecologia* 175:63-72. Mimosa pudica demonstrated habituation learning and weeks-long memory without a nervous system.
2. Simard, S. et al. (1997). "Net transfer of carbon between ectomycorrhizal tree species in the field." *Nature* 388:579-582. Carbon moved between paper birch and Douglas fir through shared mycorrhizal networks.
3. Pollack, G.H. (2013). *The Fourth Phase of Water: Beyond Solid, Liquid, and Vapor.* Ebner & Sons. Exclusion-zone (EZ) water: structured, charged, light-built water phase; professor of bioengineering, University of Washington.
4. Magnetic water and crops: Hassan et al. (2021), *Horticulturae* (MDPI), magnetically treated water/saline improved tomato germination, growth, and yield; cotton seed germination and photosynthesis gains (MDPI *Agronomy*, 2022); vegetable seedling growth gains (*Egypt. J. of Appl. Sci.*, 2019).
5. Emoto, M. (2004). *The Hidden Messages in Water.* Popular (not peer-reviewed) work claiming water crystals respond to words and intention; included as the cultural touchstone for intention-water inquiry, not as established science.
